Today

  • Coverage tested quarterly against a 1.15x floor on trailing receipts.
  • Loan reports are snapshots, published weeks later.
  • Depositors funding the loans cannot check the ratio.
  • Telemetry sees serials and health, not revenue.
  • No onchain record ties a GPU to one loan.

With Plumbline

  • The floor is re-proven before the next draw.
  • One Groth16 proof, verified in one Solana transaction.
  • The result is public: page, badge, JSON.
  • Revenue comes from zkTLS-proven payouts; individual payouts stay private.
  • The proven GPU's chip-id hash is recorded on its loan.

Three roots. One proof. One decision.

  1. 01

    The GPU is real

    NVIDIA's SPDM attestation report, signed by the H100 with P-384, checked to the NVIDIA Device Identity CA.

  2. 02

    It runs in a known enclave

    An Intel TDX quote verified to Intel's root names the VM image a lender can pin onchain.

  3. 03

    It earns enough

    Reclaim zkTLS proofs of the period's payouts. The total is public; each payout stays private.

  4. 04

    The program decides

    A RISC Zero guest checks the lender's floor and emits one 256-byte Groth16 proof. The Anchor program verifies it, clears the challenge so it cannot be replayed, and opens or refuses the draw.

Built to be checked, not trusted.

Replays refused

The lender issues a fresh challenge; the program clears it once a proof uses it. A second use fails with ZkStaleChallenge on devnet.

About 120k compute units

Our own Groth16 verifier on Solana's alt_bn128 syscalls.

32 KB of evidence, one transaction

GPU report, certificate chain, TDX quote and payout claims do not fit in 1,232 bytes. The proof does.

Straight answers.

Is this live?

The lending oracle is live on Solana devnet: a 1.15x proof was verified and its draw approved, a 1.14x draw was refused, and a replay was refused. Mainnet is refused everywhere until the verifier is audited.

What does one proof show, and what not?

That one attested GPU answered the lender's fresh challenge, ran in a known enclave, and its proven payouts cover the floor. It does not show who owns the GPU; weekly fleet sampling is planned.

What is real in the demo today?

The proofs verify NVIDIA's public sample H100 report and a published H200 TDX quote, chain-verified to their roots. Revenue claims are synthetic, signed by two test attestor keys. No TCB or revocation checks yet.

Who reads the result?

The lender's program gates the draw. The verify page, badge and JSON are free for curators, depositors and GPU networks.

What comes next on the same engine?

Sealed-eval bounties where the prize pays itself: an enclave scores entries on hidden tests and the escrow pays the wallet proven inside the score. One 0.05 SOL bounty has been paid on devnet under a test root.
