How it works

A sponsor locks a reward against a test set nobody else can read. An entrant submits a model. A TDX enclave scores the model on the sealed test set, a zero-knowledge proof checks the enclave's Intel quote on Solana, and the escrow pays the entrant's own address as soon as a verified score meets the target. Nobody, including us, sees the questions, and nobody can redirect the money.

v0 runs on localnet and devnet. The browser only reads the chain; every action below is a plumbline CLI command (plumbline/cli, run npm install there, then bin/plumbline). Add --cluster devnet for devnet. Mainnet is refused.

Sponsor: post a bounty

  1. Write your test set as a JSON suite file. Keep it on your machine. Its commitment (a SHA-256 of its canonical JSON) is what goes onchain; the questions never do.
  2. Seal it to the competition enclave. The CLI asks the enclave for a fresh attestation, checks the Intel quote and that it binds the enclave's key, then encrypts the suite to that key. It prints only the commitment, never the contents. 
    plumbline seal my-suite.json --enclave https://<enclave> \
  --enclave-pubkey <key from /health> -o sealed-suite.json
    Publish sealed-suite.json: it is ciphertext only the enclave can open. The enclave key lives only as long as that enclave instance, so re-seal if it restarts.
  3. Register the suite (optional, lets bounties require a registered suite): 
    plumbline suite register sealed-suite.json
  4. Lock the reward. The SOL moves into the bounty account, not to us. 
    plumbline bounty create --suite sealed-suite.json \
  --reward 1 --target 2/3 --deadline-slots 216000
    Optional: --pin-compose / --pin-image to accept only one enclave build; --grace-slots for how long after the deadline a late-recorded score can still claim.
  5. Wait. The first verified score that meets the target is paid automatically. Watch the bounty's leaderboard here.
  6. No winner? After the deadline plus grace, take the reward back: 
    plumbline bounty refund <bounty address>

Entrant: enter a model

  1. Pick a bounty on the list and get the sponsor's sealed-suite.json.
  2. Pin your model: a public repo id, the 40-hex commit, and the SHA-256 of each weights file.
  3. Enter, naming the Solana address that should receive the prize. The enclave downloads the pinned weights, checks every hash, scores the model on the sealed suite, and signs a statement that includes your payout address. 
    plumbline enter --enclave https://<enclave> \
  --sealed sealed-suite.json --model org/name \
  --revision <40-hex> \
  --shard model.safetensors=<sha256> \
  --payout <your address>
    You get bundle-<hash>.json. The CLI refuses a bundle whose payout is not the one you asked for.
  4. Check it yourself, here on the verify page or with 
    plumbline verify bundle-<hash>.json
  5. Hand it to a relayer (drop it in its inbox or POST /bundles). The relayer proves the quote with a Groth16 proof, records the verified score onchain, and claims every bounty it meets. It pays the fees; it cannot change where the money goes, because your payout is inside the proof.
  6. Or claim yourself once the score is recorded: 
    plumbline claim --bundle bundle-<hash>.json

Why nobody can take your prize

The payout address is a field of the statement the enclave signs. The quote's report data is a hash of that statement, the zk proof checks the quote and outputs the payout, the registry stores it in the VerifiedScore, and the escrow pays only that address. Registering someone else's public model first gets you nothing: there is no registration step to front-run.

What is real today

The programs, the escrow and the payout binding are tested on localnet with a proof from a test-root guest. A devnet bounty paid from a real Phala TDX quote needs one Phala run; see plumbline/docs/REAL-VS-PENDING.md.
