How it works

The lender issues a challenge

A 32-byte nonce goes into the loan account. The GPU must sign it, so an old report cannot be replayed.

The operator runs one prover

Inside a RISC Zero guest: NVIDIA's attestation report and its certificate chain to NVIDIA's root (the GPU is real), an Intel TDX quote verified to Intel's root (the software stack is the one we expect; when both come from the same machine, the proof binds the GPU to the enclave), and zkTLS payout proofs from two attestors (the revenue is real). The guest tests coverage and outputs pass or fail.

Solana verifies and gates the draw

The program checks the proof, the challenge, the guest image and the attestor set, then records PASS or WATCH. A draw is approved or refused with no admin key and no human sign-off.

Who it is for

GPU operators that borrow

Prove weekly, hand the public page to any lender, and stop paying for a telemetry box, a monthly audit and the spread lenders charge for revenue they cannot see.

Lenders and vault curators

Read PASS or WATCH per loan, embed the badge, pull the JSON. The covenant you already set, checked by math instead of a PDF.

GPU networks

Require a proof before a provider earns. The GPU's own signature, chain-verified to NVIDIA's root, replaces self-reported hardware.

Confidential-AI clouds

Show customers that a job ran on a real H100 inside a measured enclave, with the GPU bound to the enclave in the proof.

What the proof removes, and what it does not

Removed

  • Trusted telemetry: the GPU signs its own report.
  • The single attestor: two independent attestors per payout.
  • The admin key: the program decides from the proof alone.
  • Public revenue: only coverage in basis points and pass or fail go onchain.

Still trusted

  • NVIDIA's root certificate and Intel's root certificate.
  • The two attestors not colluding, and the zkTLS proxy model they run.
  • The RISC Zero prover and our own Groth16 verifier program (written in the window, not yet audited; mainnet after audit).
