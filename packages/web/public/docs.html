What the proof says

One RISC Zero guest (image id pinned onchain) verifies three independent roots and outputs one journal:

Root 1: the GPU is real

NVIDIA's SPDM attestation report, signed by the H100 with ECDSA P-384, and its five-certificate chain to the NVIDIA Device Identity CA. The report's nonce must equal the lender's challenge. The guest outputs hashes of the chip id and the firmware measurements.

Root 2: the enclave is the expected one

An Intel TDX DCAP v4 quote: PCK chain to Intel's SGX Root CA, QE report, attestation-key binding and the quote signature. When its report_data commits to the GPU report and the challenge, the GPU is bound to the enclave; the guest outputs the VM measurement and this bound flag, and the program can be configured to require it (the devnet demo does not). Intel TCB status, QE identity and CRLs are not yet checked.

Root 3: the revenue is real

Reclaim zkTLS claims of the period's payouts (Razorpay, Stripe, marketplace APIs), each signed by at least the threshold of attestors from the set hashed onchain (two test keys in the demo). The guest sums the period, removes duplicate payout ids, and tests revenue against debt service at the minimum coverage. Individual payout amounts and ids never leave the guest; the window's total is in the public journal.

Journal (public values, fixed layout): chip-id hash, the GPU nonce (the challenge), measurements hash, attestor-set hash, revenue source parameters hash, loan terms hash (which covers the period), total window revenue in micro-units, coverage in basis points, claim count, pass flag, VM measurement, TDX TCB SVN, bound flag. The Solana program checks the challenge, attestor set, source and terms against onchain state before accepting the seal.

Trust model

Removed

  • Trusted telemetry (the GPU signs its own report)
  • Human sign-off on each draw (the program decides from the proof; one config authority still sets the guest image, attestor set and fee)
  • Public payout detail (individual payouts, ids and tenants stay private; the window's total revenue and coverage are published)

Remaining

  • NVIDIA's and Intel's root certificates
  • The config authority, one key that sets the accepted guest image, the attestor set and threshold (the demo uses two test keys), the revenue sources and the fee
  • The attestors not colluding; Reclaim's proxy model (an attestor can still fabricate a session with the operator's cooperation)
  • The RISC Zero prover and our own Groth16 verifier program and TDX quote verifier (written in the window, not yet audited; the TDX check is structural, without TCB status, QE identity or CRLs; mainnet after audit)
  • Freshness: a proof must cover the current period or the previous one, and the program clears the challenge once a proof lands, so the same proof cannot be submitted twice

Demo evidence

The program runs end to end on a local Solana validator; the devnet deploy is pending. The demo proofs verify NVIDIA's published sample H100 report and VoltageGPU's published H200 TDX quote, both chain-verified to their roots, with revenue claims signed by two test attestor keys that anyone can derive. They are real cryptographic evidence, but the quote is not bound to the sample report (vm_bound = 0) and neither was signed against our own challenge: the demo sets the lender's challenge to the nonce already in the sample report. A verify page prints an evidence label (public or own) when the server is configured with one. A report signed inside our own enclave against our own challenge is the next step.

Pricing

  • Planned pricing: a facility fee plus 5 to 15 basis points a year on the loan, weekly proofs included, paid by the lender or passed to the borrower in the loan terms. Today the program charges one flat fee on every proof (0.0013 SOL of test SOL in the devnet demo). Verifying is free.
  • In the program today: one flat fee (fee_lamports, set by the program authority) on every proof, PASS or WATCH, collected into a treasury account at proof time. Facility fees and basis-point pricing are not in the program yet.
  • For scale: on a $16.6M loan (USD.AI's first-year average: $265M over 16 loans, August 2026), 10 basis points is $16,600 a year plus the facility fee. USD.AI already pays 150 basis points a year, about $249,000 on that loan, for a value warranty on the same collateral (reinsured by Munich Re) that never sees revenue; its own 1.15x coverage floor is tested quarterly, and Ironproof re-proves it weekly (usd.ai/insights/usdai-underwriting-and-risk-management).
  • Lenders, vault curators, GPU networks, clouds: verify pages, badges and the read API are free.

Roadmap

  • Own-enclave evidence: a report signed against our own challenge inside a confidential VM (Phala Cloud dstack, or any Intel TDX host with the NVIDIA driver in CC mode), bound in the proof.
  • Fleet Passport without a loan: prove hardware and enclave for a GPU network, no debt service.
  • Fees in USDC and x402 for the read API; several lenders reading one fleet's proofs.
  • Mainnet after the verifier audit; attestor set run with an independent operator.

Questions a careful reader asks

Why a chain at all?

Because the readers are not one lender. Vault curators and depositors in onchain credit can read PASS or WATCH without asking anyone, and the program can gate a draw on it directly. A signed PDF from a verification service cannot be composed into a draw gate.

Why zero knowledge?

The GPU signs with P-384, which no chain verifies natively, and the evidence (GPU report, certificate chain, TDX quote and payout claims) is about 32 KB against a 1,232-byte Solana transaction. The proof makes it fit in one transaction, and it is what lets individual payouts stay private while the result is public.

Does this replace the lender's monitoring vendor?

Partly. Telemetry vendors see serials, health and temperature but not revenue, and their data is trusted, not signed by the GPU. The proof covers identity, enclave and coverage; it does not cover physical custody or power.

What if the attestors collude?

Then a fake payout can be proven. That is the weakest root and it is named as such. The attestor set is pinned onchain and can be replaced by the lender's own bank-feed attestor without changing the program.
