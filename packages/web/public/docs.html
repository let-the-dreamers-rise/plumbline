What the proof says

One RISC Zero guest (image id pinned onchain) verifies three independent roots and outputs one journal:

Root 1: the GPU is real

NVIDIA's SPDM attestation report, signed by the H100 with ECDSA P-384, and its five-certificate chain to the NVIDIA Device Identity CA. The report's nonce must equal the lender's challenge. The guest outputs hashes of the chip id and the firmware measurements.

Root 2: the enclave is the expected one

An Intel TDX DCAP v4 quote: PCK chain to Intel's SGX Root CA, QE report, attestation-key binding and the quote signature. Its report_data must commit to the GPU report and the challenge, which binds the GPU to the enclave. The guest outputs the VM measurement and whether it vouched for the report.

Root 3: the revenue is real

Reclaim zkTLS claims of the period's payouts (Razorpay, Stripe, marketplace APIs), each signed by at least two attestors from the set hashed onchain. The guest sums the period, removes duplicate payout ids, and tests revenue against debt service at the minimum coverage. Amounts never leave the guest.

Journal (public values): loan id, challenge, VM measurement, attestor-set hash, revenue commitment, chip-id hash, measurements hash, coverage in basis points, period, claim count, bound flag. The Solana program re-derives every one of them from onchain state before accepting the seal.

Trust model

Removed

  • Trusted telemetry (the GPU signs its own report)
  • Single attestor (two per claim, from a set the program pins)
  • Admin key on the covenant (the program decides from the proof)
  • Public revenue (only coverage in basis points is published)

Remaining

  • NVIDIA's and Intel's root certificates
  • The attestors not colluding; Reclaim's proxy model (an attestor can still fabricate a session with the operator's cooperation)
  • The RISC Zero prover and our own Groth16 verifier program and TDX quote verifier (written in the window, not yet audited; mainnet after audit)
  • Freshness: a proof must cover the current period or the previous one, and the program clears the challenge once a proof lands, so the same proof cannot be submitted twice

Evidence on devnet today

The devnet proofs verify NVIDIA's published sample H100 report and VoltageGPU's published H200 TDX quote, both chain-verified to their roots, with revenue claims signed by two test attestors. They are real cryptographic evidence, but the quote is not bound to the sample report (vm_bound = 0) and neither was signed against our own challenge. Every verify page prints the evidence label (public or own). A report signed inside our own enclave against our own challenge is the next step.

Pricing

  • Operators: $0.25 per GPU per week, charged per proof. First four proofs free. The program collects the fee at proof time into a treasury account; on devnet the fee is set to zero.
  • Lenders, vault curators, GPU networks, clouds: verify pages, badges and the read API are free during beta. Bulk API pricing comes with mainnet.
  • Why operators pay: the gain is theirs. On a $15.8M loan (USD.AI's average), one point of rate is $158k a year, and a lender's warranty on the same collateral costs about 150 basis points a year. A 500-GPU fleet pays $6,500 a year for proofs.

Questions a careful reader asks

Why a chain at all?

Because the readers are not one lender. USD.AI's loans are onchain objects and its depositors are on Solana through Kamino; vault curators and depositors can read PASS or WATCH without asking anyone. A signed PDF from a verification service cannot be composed into a draw gate.

Why zero knowledge?

The GPU signs with P-384, which no chain verifies natively, and the evidence is megabytes. The proof makes it fit in one transaction, and it is what lets revenue stay private while the result is public.

Does this replace the lender's monitoring vendor?

Partly. Telemetry vendors see serials, health and temperature but not revenue, and their data is trusted, not signed by the GPU. The proof covers identity, enclave and coverage; it does not cover physical custody or power.

What if the attestors collude?

Then a fake payout can be proven. That is the weakest root and it is named as such. The attestor set is pinned onchain and can be replaced by the lender's own bank-feed attestor without changing the program.
