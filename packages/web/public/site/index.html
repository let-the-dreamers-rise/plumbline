How it works

Three strikes, one mark.

  1. The lender issues a challenge

    A 32-byte nonce goes into the loan account. The GPU must sign it, so an old report cannot be replayed.

  2. The operator runs one prover

    Inside a RISC Zero guest: NVIDIA's attestation report and its certificate chain to NVIDIA's root, an Intel TDX quote verified to Intel's root, and zkTLS payout proofs from two attestors. The guest tests coverage and outputs pass or fail.

  3. Solana verifies and gates the draw

    The program checks the proof, the challenge, the guest image and the attestor set, then records PASS or WATCH. A draw is approved or refused with no admin key and no human sign-off.

Questions

What a lender will ask.

Is this live?

On Solana devnet today. Mainnet comes after an audit of the verifier program, which was written in the hackathon window.

What evidence does the devnet demo use?

NVIDIA's published sample H100 report and VoltageGPU's published H200 TDX quote, both chain-verified to their roots. A report signed against our own challenge inside our own enclave is the next step, and every verify page labels it.

What goes onchain, and what stays private?

PASS or WATCH, coverage in basis points, a hash of the chip id and firmware measurements, the enclave measurement, the challenge, period, attestor-set hash and a revenue commitment. Payout amounts, payout ids, tenants, prices and the raw evidence never leave the operator's machine.

Who pays?

The operator that borrows: $0.25 per GPU per week, charged per proof, first four proofs free. Lenders, curators and networks verify for free during beta.

How long does a proof take?

97 to 108 minutes on a 4-core, 15 GB cloud VM, including the Groth16 wrap. A fleet proves once a week, so this runs in the background.

What do I still have to trust?

NVIDIA's and Intel's root certificates, the two attestors not colluding, and the RISC Zero prover and our Groth16 verifier program, which is not yet audited.
