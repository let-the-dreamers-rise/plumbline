For lenders financing GPU fleets, and the operators who borrow against them

Release the draw on a proof, not a PDF.

A lender funding a GPU fleet cannot check the collateral or the revenue behind the loan: it gets a telemetry box, a monthly PDF and a spreadsheet. With Ironproof the operator submits one zero-knowledge proof per period that its GPUs are real NVIDIA hardware, run in a measured enclave, and earned enough to cover debt service. A Solana program checks the proof and approves or refuses the next draw. Anyone can verify the result; individual payouts stay private.

A $16.6M loan, one proof a week: $16,600 a year at 10 bps plus a facility fee. USD.AI already pays 150 bps, $249,000 a year, for the warranty on the same loan. Verifying is free.

How it works

Three elements, one focal point.

  1. The lender issues a challenge

    A 32-byte nonce goes into the loan account. The GPU must sign it, so an old report cannot be replayed.

  2. The operator runs one prover

    Inside a RISC Zero guest: NVIDIA's attestation report and its certificate chain to NVIDIA's root, an Intel TDX quote verified to Intel's root, and zkTLS payout claims, each signed by k attestors from a pinned set. The guest tests coverage and outputs pass or fail.

  3. Solana verifies and gates the draw

    The program checks the proof, the challenge, the guest image and the attestor set, then records PASS or WATCH. A draw is approved or refused with no human sign-off.

Questions

What a lender will ask.

Is this live?

Not on a public cluster yet. The program, the lending flow and the proof check run end to end on a local Solana validator; the devnet deploy is pending. Mainnet comes after an audit of the verifier program, which was written in the hackathon window.

What evidence does the devnet demo use?

NVIDIA's published sample H100 report and VoltageGPU's published H200 TDX quote, both chain-verified to their roots, and revenue claims signed by two test attestor keys. The demo sets the lender's challenge to the nonce already in the sample report, and the TDX quote is not bound to that GPU. A report signed against a fresh challenge inside our own enclave is the next step.

What goes onchain, and what stays private?

Public: PASS or WATCH, coverage in basis points, the total revenue for the covenant window (in the proof's public values), the number of payout claims, a hash of the chip id and firmware measurements, the enclave measurement, the challenge, period and attestor-set hash. Coverage times debt service gives the total too. Private: individual payout amounts, payout ids, tenants, prices and the raw GPU and enclave evidence never leave the operator's machine.

Who pays?

Planned pricing: a facility fee plus 5 to 15 basis points a year on the loan, weekly proofs included, paid by the lender or passed to the borrower in the loan terms. Today the program charges one flat fee on every proof (0.0013 SOL of test SOL in the devnet demo). Verifying is free.

How long does a proof take?

97 to 108 minutes on a 4-core, 15 GB cloud VM, including the Groth16 wrap. A fleet proves once a week, so this runs in the background.

What do I still have to trust?

NVIDIA's and Intel's root certificates, the revenue attestors not colluding (the demo uses two test keys held by the developer), Intel TCB status, QE identity and CRLs (not yet checked in the guest), and the RISC Zero prover and our Groth16 verifier program, which is not yet audited.
